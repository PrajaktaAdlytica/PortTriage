Motion specification

Entrance reveals: 400–650ms, 12–24px movement, short 60–90ms stagger; body copy remains readable without JavaScript. Hover/focus: 160–220ms surface change, arrow shift capped at 4px, no universal tilt. Decorative imagery may use very shallow parallax; evidence labels never do.

Replay pin is proposed only above 1100px width and 760px height. Reserve approximately 240–320vh of scroll travel, then validate actual reading time. Map progress into four equal beats with brief readable holds. Scrubbed geometry reverses on up-scroll; actual demo decisions do not reverse with scrolling. Use a separate explicit Reset simulation control. Keep approval state separate from scene progress.

On mobile, short windows and reduced motion, use four vertical panels with full labels and manual step controls. No pin, forced smooth scrolling or horizontal overflow. A skip link exits the sequence. Keyboard focus must never land in a clipped offscreen panel. A progress label such as “2 of 4 · Evidence” is meaningful without animation.

Future implementation: React/TypeScript with route-aware GSAP ScrollTrigger cleanup; use native scrolling as the baseline. Lenis is optional after device/accessibility validation. Pin a wrapper and animate its children; invalidate measurements after media/font load and resize. See official ScrollTrigger guidance. Avoid adding WebGL solely for visual prestige.

Commercial and validation structure

Offer 1 test hypothesis: $1,500 per monitored lane per month plus $2,000 one-time feed mapping. The paid two-week concierge pilot has a separately agreed scope and fee; do not infer proration or assert that the setup charge is its price. No accepted price or active checkout exists. Offers 2–5 have no supplied prices; say “Discuss test scope.”

Qualification: VP import operations at a forwarder or high-volume importer; repeated exceptions on a named lane; exception coordinator involved; named data/TMS owner. Do not qualify merely by tracked-container count. Begin with Port Desk or Port Review, not all five.

Interview guide

  1. Walk through the last exception on this lane. Which promise or handoff was threatened?
  2. Which port-call and container facts differed, and when did you observe each?
  3. Where did you find the evidence? Which sources could you legally share?
  4. What does your current TMS or visibility provider already do for this exception?
  5. Who owned the next action, and where was that ownership recorded?
  6. How many coordinator minutes and manual handoffs did the case require?
  7. Who may contact the carrier, terminal, dispatcher and customer?
  8. What evidence distinguishes preventable delay from delay outside your control?
  9. Would you permit a redacted sample and a two-week comparison with the current queue?
  10. What result, fee and named integration approver would support a signed paid pilot?

Objection notes

  • “We already use project44.” Compare actual exception ownership, approvals, handoffs and audit functionality in a demo. Retain the incumbent when it already solves the job; do not claim unverified whitespace.
  • “Our TMS can do this.” Run the same redacted cases through the existing queue and proposed workflow. Integration cost and duplicate work count against PortTriage.
  • “Can you see terminal holds?” Only appropriately entitled evidence can establish status. Standards alone do not provide access; otherwise display unknown.
  • “Will this reduce demurrage?” Test defensible attribution and avoidable cost; no guaranteed savings.
  • “Can the agent reschedule or notify automatically?” The initial concept prepares reviewed drafts; no automatic appointment change or production notice.
  • “Why pay before integrations exist?” A scoped concierge pilot tests the operational outcome before engineering investment; price acceptance remains unvalidated.
